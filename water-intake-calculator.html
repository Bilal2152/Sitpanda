Why the "8 Glasses a Day" Rule is a Myth

You have probably heard that you should drink 8 glasses of water a day. While that's a good general goal, your actual needs depend on your body size, how much you sweat, and the weather. A 50kg sedentary person needs much less water than a 90kg athlete training in the summer.

Factors That Increase Water Needs

  • Exercise: When you sweat, you lose water and electrolytes. You should drink an extra 1.5 to 2.5 cups (350-600ml) of water for every 30 minutes of exercise.
  • Environment: Hot or humid weather can make you sweat more, requiring additional fluid intake. High altitudes and indoor heating can also increase water loss.
  • Illness: Fever, vomiting, and diarrhea cause massive fluid loss. Doctors often recommend increasing water intake during these times.

Signs of Dehydration

Thirst is the body's natural alarm system, but it's often a late indicator. Other signs include dark yellow urine, fatigue, dizziness, and dry mouth. If you are well-hydrated, your urine should be pale yellow and clear.
