What is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the fixed amount you pay to a bank or lender each month to repay your loan. The EMI consists of two parts: the principal amount (the money you borrowed) and the interest (the cost of borrowing).

EMI Formula Used

We use the standard reducing-balance formula used by banks globally:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

  • P = Principal loan amount
  • r = Monthly interest rate (Annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
  • n = Number of monthly instalments (Years × 12)

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a longer tenure reduce my EMI? Yes, a longer tenure means smaller monthly EMIs. However, you will end up paying more total interest over the life of the loan.

Is EMI fixed or variable? If you have a fixed-rate loan, your EMI remains constant. For floating-rate loans, the EMI can change if the benchmark interest rate changes.

Can I prepay my loan? Yes. Prepaying reduces your principal amount, which either lowers your EMI or shortens your tenure. Check with your bank for prepayment penalties.
