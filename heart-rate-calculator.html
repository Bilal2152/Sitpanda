What are Heart Rate Zones?

Heart rate zones are ranges of beats per minute (BPM) that correspond to different levels of exercise intensity. By training in specific zones, you can target different physiological adaptations—like burning fat (Zone 2) or improving speed (Zone 4).

Karvonen Formula vs. Simple Percentage

Many basic calculators simply take your Max HR and multiply it by a percentage (e.g., 70% of Max HR). However, the Karvonen formula is considered more accurate because it factors in your Resting Heart Rate (RHR). A fit person with a low RHR will have different zones than an unfit person of the same age with a high RHR.

The 5 Zones Explained

  • Zone 1 (50–60%): Very light. Good for warm-ups and recovery days.
  • Zone 2 (60–70%): Light. The "Fat Burn" zone. You can hold a conversation here.
  • Zone 3 (70–80%): Moderate. Improves aerobic capacity and endurance.
  • Zone 4 (80–90%): Hard. Anaerobic training for speed and power.
  • Zone 5 (90–100%): Maximum. Short bursts only. Trains your VO2 max.
