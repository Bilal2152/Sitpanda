Compound Interest Formula Used

The calculator uses the standard compound interest formula:

A = P × (1 + r/n)n×t

  • A = Future value (final amount)
  • P = Principal (initial investment)
  • r = Annual interest rate (as a decimal)
  • n = Number of compounding periods per year
  • t = Time in years

For monthly contributions, the calculator uses the future value of an annuity formula and adds it to the principal growth.

Simple vs Compound Interest — What’s the Difference?

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal. Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus previously earned interest — so your money grows faster over time.

  • Simple interest formula: SI = P × r × t
  • Compound interest formula: A = P(1 + r/n)nt

Frequently Asked Questions (Global)

Is this compound interest calculator free? Yes. All SitPanda calculators are 100% free with no signup required.

Does it work for any country? Yes. Select your country from the dropdown to load typical local rates and compounding conventions, or enter your own numbers manually. The calculator supports all major currencies.

What is the difference between nominal and effective annual rate? The nominal rate is the stated annual rate. The effective annual rate (EAR) accounts for compounding within the year — it is always higher than or equal to the nominal rate when compounding occurs more than once a year. The calculator shows the EAR in the results.

How do monthly contributions affect my returns? Monthly contributions add to your principal over time, and each contribution starts earning compound interest from the month it is added. This is why starting early and contributing regularly is so powerful.

Which compounding frequency gives the highest return? More frequent compounding produces higher returns: daily > monthly > quarterly > semi-annually > annually. However, the difference between monthly and daily is usually small for typical savings rates.

What is the Rule of 72? Divide 72 by your annual interest rate to estimate how many years it will take for your money to double. At 8%, money doubles in roughly 9 years.

What is the difference between APR and APY? APR (Annual Percentage Rate) is the nominal rate without compounding. APY (Annual Percentage Yield) includes the effect of compounding. A 5% APR compounded monthly gives a 5.12% APY.

Does inflation affect compound interest? Yes. Inflation reduces purchasing power. At 3% inflation, the cost of living doubles in about 24 years. Your investments need to outpace inflation to maintain real value.
