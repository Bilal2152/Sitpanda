What is Ideal Body Weight (IBW)?

Ideal Body Weight (IBW) is a medical concept that estimates the healthiest weight for a person based on their height and gender. It was originally created by the insurance industry and pharmacologists to determine safe medication dosages, but today it is widely used as a general health benchmark.

Which Formula is Most Accurate?

There is no single "perfect" formula. Each formula was developed using different population data:

  • Robinson (1983): Often considered the most accurate for modern populations.
  • Miller (1983): Designed to be more accurate for people with lower body weights.
  • Devine (1974): The most widely used formula in clinical settings (hospitals).
  • Hamwi (1964): An older formula that tends to give slightly higher ideal weights.

Why the Healthy BMI Range Matters

While the formulas above give a single number, the Healthy BMI Range (18.5 to 24.9) gives you a "safe zone." Being anywhere within this range is generally considered healthy. Muscle mass, bone structure, and age can make your personal ideal weight different from a formula's calculation.
