What is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is the interest you earn on both your original money and on the interest you've accumulated over time. It is often called "interest on interest."

For example, if you invest $1,000 at a 10% annual return, you earn $100 in the first year. In the second year, you earn 10% on $1,100, which is $110. Over decades, this snowball effect can turn small investments into significant wealth.

Compound Interest Formula

The calculator uses the standard compound interest formula with optional monthly contributions:

A = P(1 + r/n)nt + PMT × [((1 + r/n)nt − 1) ÷ (r/n)]
  • A = Future Value of the investment
  • P = Initial Principal
  • PMT = Monthly Contribution
  • r = Annual Interest Rate (as a decimal)
  • n = Number of times interest is compounded per year
  • t = Time in years

Frequently Asked Questions

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is the interest you earn on both your original money and on the interest you've accumulated over time. It's often called 'interest on interest'.

How does compounding frequency affect my investment?

The more frequently interest is compounded (e.g., monthly vs annually), the faster your investment grows because you start earning interest on the interest sooner.

Is this calculator adjusted for inflation?

No. This calculator shows the nominal future value. To see the "real" value adjusted for inflation, you would need to subtract the expected inflation rate from your interest rate.
