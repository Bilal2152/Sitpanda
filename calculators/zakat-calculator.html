What is Zakat?

Zakat is one of the Five Pillars of Islam. It is an obligatory charitable contribution, typically 2.5% of a Muslim's accumulated wealth, paid annually to those in need. The word "Zakat" literally means "to cleanse" — Muslims believe that paying Zakat purifies their wealth and pleases Allah SWT.

Zakat is only due if your wealth exceeds a minimum threshold called Nisab, and if you have held that wealth for one full lunar year (Hawl).

What is Nisab?

Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth a Muslim must possess before they become eligible to pay Zakat. The Nisab was set by Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) at a rate equivalent to 87.48 grams of gold or 612.36 grams of silver.

Because the prices of gold and silver fluctuate daily, the Nisab value in your local currency also changes. Many scholars recommend using the silver Nisab because it is almost always a lower threshold than gold. This allows more people to be eligible to pay Zakat, which means more help for those in need.

Which Assets Are Subject to Zakat?

Only assets that have the potential to grow or increase are subject to Zakat. These include:

  • Cash & Bank Balances: Savings, current accounts, and cash in hand.
  • Gold, Silver & Precious Metals: In any form (jewelry, coins, bars).
  • Business Assets: Trading stock, inventory, and receivables.
  • Investments: Stocks, shares, and mutual funds (at current market value).
  • Loans Given: Money you have lent to others that you expect to be repaid.

Not subject to Zakat: Your primary residence, personal car, furniture, and other fixed assets used for personal use.
