What is Zakat?

Zakat is one of the Five Pillars of Islam. It is an obligatory charitable contribution, typically 2.5% of a Muslim's accumulated wealth, paid annually to those in need.

Zakat is only due if your wealth exceeds a minimum threshold called Nisab, and if you have held that wealth for one full lunar year (Hawl).

How is Zakat Calculated?

The calculation is straightforward:

Zakat = 2.5% × (Total Assets − Liabilities)
  • Total Assets: Cash, bank balances, gold, silver, stocks, and business inventory.
  • Liabilities: Debts you owe that are due immediately.
  • Nisab: The minimum threshold. If your net wealth is below this, Zakat is not due.

What is Nisab?

Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth a Muslim must have before being liable for Zakat. There are two standards:

  • Gold Standard: 87.48 grams (7.5 tola) of gold.
  • Silver Standard: 612.36 grams (52.5 tola) of silver.

Most scholars recommend using the silver standard for the poor, as it results in more people being eligible to receive Zakat and more Zakat being distributed.
