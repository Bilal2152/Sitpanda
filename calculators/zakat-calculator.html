What is Zakat?

Zakat is one of the Five Pillars of Islam. It is an obligatory charitable contribution, typically 2.5% of a Muslim's accumulated wealth, paid annually to those in need. The word "Zakat" literally means "to cleanse" — Muslims believe that paying Zakat purifies their wealth and pleases Allah SWT [citation:4].

Zakat is only due if your wealth exceeds a minimum threshold called Nisab, and if you have held that wealth for one full lunar year (Hawl).

What is Nisab?

Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth a Muslim must possess before they become eligible to pay Zakat. The Nisab was set by Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) at a rate equivalent to 87.48 grams of gold or 612.36 grams of silver [citation:4].

Gold Nisab vs Silver Nisab

Because the prices of gold and silver fluctuate daily, the Nisab value in your local currency also changes. Many scholars recommend using the silver Nisab because it is almost always a lower threshold than gold. This allows more people to be eligible to pay Zakat, which means more help for those in need [citation:8].

However, if your wealth consists entirely of gold, you must use the gold Nisab. Similarly, if it consists entirely of silver, use the silver Nisab. For mixed assets, the majority of scholars recommend the silver standard [citation:4].

Which Assets Are Subject to Zakat?

Only assets that have the potential to grow or increase are subject to Zakat. These include [citation:4]:

  • Cash & Bank Balances: Savings, current accounts, and cash in hand.
  • Gold & Silver: In any form (jewelry, coins, bars) — according to most scholars.
  • Business Assets: Trading stock, inventory, and receivables.
  • Investments: Stocks, shares, and mutual funds (at current market value).
  • Loans Given: Money you have lent to others that you expect to be repaid.

Not subject to Zakat: Your primary residence, personal car, furniture, and other fixed assets used for personal use.

When is Zakat Due?

Zakat becomes obligatory once a full lunar year (approximately 354 days) has passed since your wealth first reached or exceeded the Nisab. This date is your personal "Zakat anniversary" or "seed date." You calculate and pay Zakat on that date each year, regardless of fluctuations in your wealth during the year [citation:8].

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Zakat and Zakat al-Fitr?

Zakat (al-Mal) is the annual 2.5% on wealth, due once a lunar year has passed. Zakat al-Fitr is a smaller, separate charity due before Eid prayer at the end of Ramadan, and it is not subject to the Nisab threshold.

Can I deduct my debts from my Zakatable wealth?

Yes. Debts you owe that are due immediately can be deducted from your total assets. However, long-term debts like a mortgage are generally not deducted in full; only the current year's payments are considered [citation:8].

Do I pay Zakat on my house and car?

No. Your primary residence, personal car, and household furniture are not subject to Zakat. Zakat is only on wealth that is saved or invested, not on assets you use for daily living [citation:4].
